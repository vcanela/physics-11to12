1. Units and prefixes

Every physics quantity is a number and a unit. Before you substitute into any formula, convert everything to SI units: metres (m), kilograms (kg), seconds (s), amperes (A).

Prefixes are shorthand for powers of ten:

PrefixSymbolMultiply byExample
gigaG1092 GHz = 2 × 109 Hz
megaM1065 MW = 5 × 106 W
kilok1033 km = 3000 m
centic10−245 cm = 0.45 m
millim10−320 mA = 0.020 A
microµ10−68 µs = 8 × 10−6 s
nanon10−9600 nm = 6.00 × 10−7 m

Two traps catch everyone:

  • Mass is in kilograms, not grams. 250 g is 0.250 kg.
  • Speed is in m s−1, not km/h. To convert km/h to m s−1, divide by 3.6. To go back, multiply by 3.6.

In NCEA, units are written with negative powers: m s−1 means metres per second, and m s−2 means metres per second squared.

A car travels at 90 km/h. What is its speed in m s−1?

  1. 90 km/h = 90 000 m in 3600 s 1 km = 1000 m, 1 h = 3600 s
  2. v = 90 0003600 = 25 m s−1
  3. Check with the shortcut: 903.6 = 25 ✓

2. Scientific notation

Physics deals with very large and very small numbers. Scientific notation writes them as

a × 10n, where 1 ≤ a < 10

  • A big number has a positive power: 6 400 000 m = 6.4 × 106 m.
  • A small number has a negative power: 0.000 25 s = 2.5 × 10−4 s.
  • The power tells you how many places the decimal point moves.

On your calculator, use the ×10x or EXP button, not “× 10 ^”. Typing 3 × 10 ^ 8 ÷ 2 × 10 ^ 4 does not do what you expect, because the calculator works left to right. The EXP button keeps each number together as one block.

Worked example

Light travels at 3.0 × 108 m s−1. The Sun is 1.5 × 1011 m from Earth. How long does sunlight take to reach us?

  1. v = dt, so t = dv
  2. t = 1.5 × 10113.0 × 108 divide the numbers, subtract the powers
  3. t = 0.5 × 103 = 5.0 × 102 s about 8 minutes

3. Significant figures

Significant figures (s.f.) show how precisely a value is known. The rules for counting them:

  • All non-zero digits count: 4.27 has 3 s.f.
  • Zeros between other digits count: 4.007 has 4 s.f.
  • Zeros at the start never count: 0.0052 has 2 s.f.
  • Zeros at the end, after a decimal point count: 3.50 has 3 s.f.
  • Zeros at the end of a whole number are unclear: 3000 could be 1 or 4 s.f. Scientific notation removes the doubt: 3.0 × 103 has 2 s.f.

For answers: give your final answer to the same number of significant figures as the least precise value in the question. Usually that is 2 or 3 s.f. Keep all the digits in your calculator while working, and round only once, at the end.

Worked example

A runner covers 125 m in 4.6 s. Calculate their average speed.

  1. v = dt = 1254.6 = 27.1739… calculator display
  2. 125 has 3 s.f.; 4.6 has 2 s.f. The least precise is 2 s.f.
  3. v = 27 m s−1

4. Rearranging formulae

Most physics questions give you a formula with the unknown in the wrong place. To rearrange, do the same thing to both sides until the unknown is on its own. Undo operations in reverse order:

  • Multiplied by something? Divide both sides by it.
  • Divided by something? Multiply both sides by it.
  • Squared? Take the square root of both sides.

Rearrange first, then substitute numbers. It is easier to follow, and easier to find your mistakes.

Worked example

A 2.0 kg ball has 100 J of kinetic energy. How fast is it moving?

  1. Ek = 12mv2
  2. 2Ek = mv2 multiply both sides by 2
  3. 2Ekm = v2 divide both sides by m
  4. v = √(2Ekm) square root both sides
  5. v = √(2 × 1002.0) = √100 = 10 m s−1

In these questions, use g = 10 m s−2.

5. Gradient and area

Two things you can read from a graph appear again and again in physics.

Gradient is how steep the line is:

gradient = riserun = ΔyΔx

The gradient has units: the y unit divided by the x unit. That tells you what it means.

Area under the line is the region between the line and the x axis. Its units are the y unit multiplied by the x unit. Split it into rectangles and triangles (area of a triangle = 12 × base × height).

GraphGradient givesArea gives
distance against timespeed (m s−1)nothing useful
speed against timeacceleration (m s−2)distance travelled (m)

Worked example

(a) Find the car’s acceleration in the first 4 s. (b) Find the total distance travelled in 10 s.

  1. (a) a = gradient = ΔvΔt = 12 − 04 − 0 = 3.0 m s−2
  2. (b) Area of triangle (0 to 4 s) = 12 × 4 × 12 = 24 m
  3. Area of rectangle (4 to 10 s) = 6 × 12 = 72 m
  4. Total distance = 24 + 72 = 96 m

6. Trigonometry and vectors

Some quantities only have a size, such as mass or time. These are scalars. Others have a size and a direction, such as velocity, force and displacement. These are vectors. At Level 2 you will combine vectors and split them into parts, and right-angle triangles are the tool for both.

  • Pythagoras: hypotenuse2 = adjacent2 + opposite2
  • SOH: sin θ = oppositehypotenuse
  • CAH: cos θ = adjacenthypotenuse
  • TOA: tan θ = oppositeadjacent
  • To find an angle, use the inverse button: θ = tan−1(oppositeadjacent), and similarly for sin−1 and cos−1.

Check your calculator is in degrees (a small D or DEG on the screen). This is the most common reason for a wrong answer.

Worked example 1: combining two vectors

A boat heads north at 4.0 m s−1 while a current pushes it east at 3.0 m s−1. Find its actual speed and direction.

  1. Draw the two vectors tip to tail. They form a right-angle triangle.
  2. speed = √(4.02 + 3.02) = √25 = 5.0 m s−1
  3. θ = tan−1(3.04.0) = 36.9° angle measured from north
  4. The boat moves at 5.0 m s−1, 37° east of north.

Worked example 2: splitting a vector into components

A rope pulls a sledge with a force of 50 N at 30° above the horizontal. How much of the force acts horizontally, and how much vertically?

  1. The 50 N force is the hypotenuse.
  2. horizontal = 50 × cos 30° = 43.3 = 43 N adjacent side, so CAH
  3. vertical = 50 × sin 30° = 25 N opposite side, so SOH
